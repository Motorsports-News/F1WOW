The 20-Pole Club: Every Member a Champion - Except Charles Leclerc

A stat graphic from F1GuyDan, posted by @f1wow days before Zandvoort, reduces Charles Leclerc's career to one brutal line: every driver in Formula 1 history with more than 20 pole positions became world champion. Every driver but one.

By F1wow Team 5 min read

ZANDVOORT WEEK, August 18 — With Formula 1 idle since Hungary, the summer-break discourse has found its favourite target. A graphic shared by @f1wow, created by stats account F1GuyDan, lays out the line every Ferrari fan knows by heart: "14 of the 15 drivers to have more than 20 pole positions have went on to become world champion. Charles Leclerc (27 poles) is the only driver to not. Will he ever become a world champion?" The post drew a comment section 22 deep within a day — and the answer, mostly, was no.

The Stat, and Why It Lands

Plenty of F1 stats are noise. This one is surgical. Reaching 20 career pole positions is so difficult that only a handful of drivers per generation manage it — and across 76 seasons of the world championship, the list of men who got there reads like a hall of fame ballot. Hamilton and Schumacher. Senna and Clark. Fangio and Prost. Every single one converted that one-lap dominance into a world title.

Every single one except Charles Leclerc. The Monegasque, in his ninth season and eighth at Ferrari, sits sixth on the all-time pole list — ahead of Jim Clark, Alain Prost, Nigel Mansell and Juan Manuel Fangio — yet his championship trophy cabinet remains empty. The graphic's count of "27 poles" has if anything fallen behind reality: by championship data through the summer break, Leclerc's career tally already stands at 34 qualifying top spots.

The 20-Pole Club in Full

Here is the club the graphic refers to — every driver in Formula 1 history with more than 20 career pole positions, with titles for each. One note: since the graphic was made, Lando Norris has crossed the threshold as well, taking the membership to 16. As the reigning world champion, he joins the winners' side — and the stat's conclusion is unchanged. Leclerc is still the only member never to be crowned.

Below the threshold, the nearest champion is Kimi Räikkönen, who stopped at 19 poles — one short of the club Leclerc dwarfs. That is the company the stat keeps: getting to 20 poles almost always means you were, at some point, the fastest man in the fastest car. Which is precisely the defence Leclerc's supporters keep making.

The Qualifying King Without a Crown

Leclerc's path to the wrong end of this record has been extraordinary from the start. Promoted to Ferrari for 2019 after a single Sauber season, he took six poles that year — more than any driver not named Hamilton that season — and has kept stacking them: two in 2021, a career-best nine in 2022, seven in 2023, five in 2024. That 2022 campaign remains the great what-if: three wins, nine poles and a season-long title fight that ended with him as runner-up to Max Verstappen, 308 points to 454.

The wins column is where the symmetry breaks. Nine career victories — including this season's British Grand Prix at Silverstone — against 34 poles. For comparison, every other member of the club converted poles to wins at a far healthier rate, because they spent their peak pole-taking years in cars that could also win on Sunday. Leclerc has never had both at once: his most dominant qualifying seasons came in Ferraris that were second- or third-fastest in race trim.

What 2026 Says: Saturday Is Still His

The pattern is running at full strength this season. Ferrari's SF-26 has been arguably the best car through the corners all year, and Leclerc has cashed that in on Saturdays: three sprint-shootout poles — in China, Japan and Monaco — while the grand prix poles went elsewhere (Kimi Antonelli six, George Russell four, Lando Norris one). Add the Silverstone win and fourth place in the championship on 138 points, and the season is a microcosm of the career: genuinely fast, genuinely good, and still 81 points behind Antonelli with 12 of 23 rounds left.

The obstacle is the one Ferrari has been fighting all year — a straight-line deficit to Mercedes estimated at around 20bhp, the exact gap the Scuderia's ADUO engine upgrade, worth a reported 15bhp, is meant to close at Monza on September 6. (We covered the full two-phase recovery plan in our Monza ADUO engine report.) Until the car catches up, the stat's question mark stays exactly where it is.

The Comment Section's Verdict

If the graphic poses the question, the replies answer it bleakly. The top-liked comment, on 67 likes, argues Leclerc has simply never had the machinery: "Because he drags non-championship cars to the pole, he never had the fastest car throughout his whole career." Another, on 39 likes, reaches for historical parallels: "He will end up like DC or Massa... always a big what-if... he had his chance to fight for the title in 2022... he is just too loyal to Ferrari."

The bleakest replies are the shortest. To the question "will he ever become a world champion?", the first answer in the thread was two words: "Not at Ferrari." Another fan put it more colorfully: Leclerc drags slow cars to pole, "and then you got a Red Bull from 2023 or a Mercedes as opponent who just passes you on the first straight of the race." Loyalty, the consensus runs, is the trap.

What to Watch: History Says There Is Time

For every gloved-punch stat there is a counterweight. Nigel Mansell won his only title at 39, in 1992, after a career of near-misses. Nico Rosberg ground out his championship in 2016 in his eleventh season, then walked away. Leclerc is 28 — he turns 29 in October — and in his ninth campaign. On the calendar alone, he has time; what he needs is a car that wins on Sundays as well as it shines on Saturdays.

The next data points arrive fast. The season resumes at the Dutch Grand Prix, August 21–23 — the final Zandvoort race under the current contract, and a sprint weekend where Ferrari's aero strength plays. Then comes Monza on September 6 and the ADUO engine's moment of truth. Track times and live results are on our Race Hub, the run-in is on the 2026 race calendar, and the live points table is always on our championship standings page. The 20-pole club's only outlier is about to find out if 2026 is the year the asterisk finally comes off.
