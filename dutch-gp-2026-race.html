Two From Two: Norris Rules Zandvoort as Verstappen's Home Race Ends in the Wall

Lando Norris backed up his Hungarian Grand Prix win with pole and victory at a rain-tinged, red-flagged Dutch GP — but Zandvoort's farewell weekend will be remembered for Max Verstappen's lap-one crash, and for the saves, penalties and team orders that followed.

By F1wow Team 11 min read

ZANDVOORT, August 23 — Lando Norris made it two Grand Prix wins in eight days, adding the Dutch Grand Prix to his Hungarian victory with a performance that had a bit of everything: pole by a tenth, a lost lead, a 26-minute red flag, and a late-race pass for the win. Max Verstappen's home race lasted less than a lap before a heavy crash brought out the red flags. Kimi Antonelli led much of the distance and finished second, Fernando Alonso dragged Aston Martin into the points from eighteenth, and Norris crossed the line 11.536 seconds clear of a Mercedes that had been swapped around by its own team in the closing stages.

A Farewell Weekend Turned on Its Head

Zandvoort's final scheduled weekend on the Formula 1 calendar had already delivered plenty before a wheel was turned in anger: Max Verstappen arrived having just put pen to paper on a new Red Bull deal that runs to 2030, the Sprint format returned to the Dutch seaside, and the orange grandstands were full for their hero one more time.

Sunday threatened to steal the show entirely. Heavy rain fell in the build-up — Liam Lawson had a moment through the gravel on the drive to the grid — and the formation lap ran behind the Safety Car with a mix of intermediates and full wets still bolted on. By the time the lights went out, the showers had eased, the racing line had dried, and the field had switched to slicks: most of the pack on softs, the top four plus Gasly, Colapinto and Ocon on mediums, and both Cadillacs on hards. Sergio Pérez would start from the pit lane after set-up changes under parc fermé conditions.

Qualifying: Norris Takes Pole by 0.102s

Saturday's session for the Grand Prix was a knife-fight at the top. Norris claimed pole with a 1:11.163, just 0.102s ahead of George Russell, with Kimi Antonelli another three hundredths back — the top three covered by 0.133s. Oscar Piastri made it two McLarens in the top four, ahead of both Ferraris.

The local hero's story was very different: Verstappen could manage only seventh, 0.455s off pole, in front of a home crowd that has watched him win here before. Liam Lawson, standing in at Red Bull this weekend, out-qualified nobody ahead of him but did edge Gabriel Bortoleto's excellent ninth and Arvid Lindblad in tenth. Further back, Fernando Alonso lined up eighteenth, Nico Hülkenberg thirteenth, and both Cadillacs anchored the rear of the field.

Sprint: Russell Controls It From the Front

The weekend's first points went to Mercedes. Russell took Sprint Shootout pole with a 1:11.567, just 0.041s ahead of Norris, and converted it into a controlled Sprint victory by 1.360s over Charles Leclerc — the Ferrari having demoted Norris with a move into Turn 1 on lap 18 as the McLaren's deployment ran out. Antonelli jumped Piastri at the start and came home fourth; Verstappen took sixth, three points that would turn out to be his only score of the entire weekend.

Further down, Bortoleto missed the final point by under two seconds in ninth, Hülkenberg dropped to twenty-second and last after starting fourteenth, and Alonso could make only eighteenth stick from twenty-second on the Sprint grid. The win trimmed Russell's championship deficit to his own teammate — a subplot that would twist again on Sunday.

Lap One: The Crash That Stopped the Race

When the five lights went out, Norris held his lead and Antonelli launched better than Russell to take second into the first corner. Leclerc demoted Piastri to fourth, and the two Red Bulls of Verstappen and Lawson raced wheel-to-wheel through the opening exchanges. Then, as Verstappen came off the final banking onto the main straight — the inside of the corner still damp in places from the pre-race showers — he dipped a wheel across the white line, the rear snapped away, and the Red Bull hit the wall heavily, shedding a wheel and sliding back across the circuit before coming to rest.

The Dutchman was quickly on the radio — "Yeah, that's it — sorry," he told the team, per Motorsport.com's race coverage. He emerged unscathed and later reported he was fine, noting that a more frontal impact "is actually a bit better than a sideways one" — small comfort at his home race, on a weekend built around his new contract, in what is scheduled to be Zandvoort's final Grand Prix for the foreseeable future. With debris strewn across the track, the red flags came out, and the field filed through the pit lane for a free tyre change during a suspension that lasted around 26 minutes.

Two Audis survived moments of their own in the chaos. Gabriel Bortoleto, running directly behind the crash, spun a full 360 degrees but kept his car out of the barriers and going — dropping to eighteenth. "The end of the first lap was quite scary. I saw what was happening ahead of me and just reacted instinctively," said Nico Hülkenberg, who swerved clear of his own spinning teammate as the field scattered around the stricken Red Bull. Both saves would prove enormously valuable by the end of the afternoon.

There was one more casualty before the restart: Ollie Bearman's Haas stopped with a technical issue between Turns 10 and 11 en route to the grid, forcing an extra formation lap while it was cleared. The race resumed at 15:33 local time with a standing start, in the order from the end of lap one.

The Restart: Antonelli's Break, Norris's Grind

Antonelli's getaway at the restart was superb — he swept straight past Norris into the lead. Piastri also jumped Russell for third, dropping the Sprint winner into the clutches of Leclerc, and for a long stretch the race had a familiar shape: a Mercedes managing a gap, a McLaren stalking it.

Antonelli held that gap around 1.4s — just enough to keep Norris out of Overtake Mode range — while the McLarens of Norris reported genuine struggle. "I'm pace zero — I've got nothing else," Norris said over the radio around lap 16, as reported in the official race coverage. The pit window then reshuffled everything: Russell was the first of the leaders to stop on lap 17 for hards, Piastri followed a lap later with a slow stop that cost him, and Antonelli and Norris covered each other around lap 21, both losing time in the pit lane. Hamilton, the last of the frontrunners to stop, cycled through to lead the race before his own stop on lap 25.

The midfield was busy too: a Gasly lock-up let Lindblad through, Hülkenberg picked off the Racing Bulls, and Tsunoda also found a way past Gasly. By half distance the order read Antonelli, Norris, Russell, Leclerc, Hamilton, Piastri, Lawson, Hülkenberg, Tsunoda, Gasly — with Leclerc looking the fastest Ferrari, having gone around the outside of Piastri for fourth before Hamilton also found a way past the Australian.

The Final Third: Norris Strikes, Mercedes Shuffle

The second round of stops brought the race to the boil. Antonelli and Russell pitted around lap 40; Ferrari's box descended into friction as Hamilton, on much fresher tyres, crawled all over Leclerc — who initially ignored a call to pit, then stopped a lap later for a slow service that dropped him behind his teammate. "Great waste of time there, guys," Hamilton said sarcastically over the radio, according to the official coverage, although the stop in fact released him into clean air.

Norris made his final stop on lap 47 and rejoined third, five seconds behind Antonelli. Three laps later the gap was 1.4s. By lap 52 he was on the Mercedes' gearbox as Antonelli fought dying rear tyres, and though the Italian used Overtake Mode — available to him within a second of the yet-to-stop Hamilton — to defend, Norris swept ahead into Turn 1 and took the race lead from Hamilton moments later. Hamilton's response as the McLaren cleared off: "Are you using me as a guinea pig?" — the seven-time champion wanting to pit, per Motorsport.com.

A Virtual Safety Car for Esteban Ocon's stopped Haas then set up the endgame: Antonelli dived in for softs and rejoined behind Russell; Norris asked "Do you think we need to take it? I'm a bit worried now" — but the VSC ended before McLaren could act. When racing resumed on lap 58, Norris led Russell by 6.8s with Antonelli a further 5.6s back.

The final twist came on lap 65. Antonelli, on his fresh softs, had closed to within 2.4s of Russell when Mercedes inverted its cars, letting the championship leader through in pursuit of Norris. Russell complied — with a slightly disgruntled radio reply, noting the team "may have lost a double podium" as Hamilton closed in. Up front, none of it mattered: Norris was untouchable, and took the flag 11.536s clear. Russell hung onto third by less than a second from Hamilton, with Leclerc only half a second further back and Piastri a distant sixth. Charles Leclerc at least banked the fastest lap, a 1:14.230 set on lap 60.

"I'm incredibly happy to take the win at the final race here in Zandvoort, but it was a real fight. For the first half of the race, especially on the Soft tyre, I honestly didn't think a win was possible. I struggled a lot with the pace and balance, and it felt like a bit of a repeat of Saturday's struggles. However, once we put the Hard tyres on, the car came alive." Lando Norris, McLaren race winner

Antonelli saw both sides of his afternoon. "I am disappointed to have led so many laps today and not win. Unfortunately, the pace wasn't quite there, and that's a shame. P2 was ultimately the maximum we could have done, though, so we must be happy coming away with that," the championship leader said — a second straight Dutch GP podium that actually extended his points lead.

Drive of the Day: Alonso's One-Stop Rescue

While the frontrunners burned through tyre sets, Fernando Alonso ran the race like a chess problem. From eighteenth on the grid, he made his single — and only — pit stop of the afternoon on lap 34 and simply kept going, picking off the multi-stoppers as they cycled through the pit lane. Eighteenth became ninth at the flag — two points, and a reminder that the old master has lost none of his racecraft. "It's great to finish in the points here in Zandvoort. I wasn't expecting it, so it's a credit to the team for maximising everything we had," Alonso said — while making clear the car still has "some driveability issues" to fix, and that scoring points is now "mandatory" for the team every weekend.

Those two points matter more than they sound: Alonso has now scored three of the three points Aston Martin has managed all season, with Lance Stroll still on zero and both cars off the bottom of the constructors' table only thanks to Cadillac's blank sheet. It is a brutal picture of where the team stands — and how much Alonso is extracting from it.

Two rows back, the other veterans of the lap-one incident were rewarded for their saves. Hülkenberg, who swerved clear of his spinning teammate, followed up his first points of the season in Hungary with eighth place here — back-to-back scoring weekends that have doubled Audi's constructors' tally. And Bortoleto, who kept his 360-degree spin out of the wall, recovered from the back of the field to thirteenth at the flag, three stops and all — though the rookie felt the weekend had slipped away earlier than his moment of fame: "It's been a bit of a disappointing end to the weekend for us. We had some issues at the start that unfortunately didn't allow us to maximise our grid position."

The Stewards' Notebook

  • Lindblad & Colapinto — drive-through penalties during the race for yellow-flag infringements committed around the red-flag period.
  • Liam Lawson — a 10-second time penalty for a late-race yellow-flag infringement; he kept seventh regardless.
  • Franco Colapinto — a further 10-second time penalty for a yellow-flag infringement, capping a difficult day in fourteenth.
  • Carlos Sainz — a 10-second time penalty for causing a collision in the clash between the two Williams cars; the debris brought out a late Virtual Safety Car, and Albon's car later retired.
  • Bortoleto & Bearman — both investigated after the race for leaving the pit exit on a red light during the red-flag period.

What It Means for the Championship

The title math barely moved at the top — and moved plenty underneath it. Antonelli's second place adds to a championship lead that now stands at 59 points over his own teammate, with Russell and Hamilton tied on 183 behind him. Norris's 31-point weekend (Sprint third, Grand Prix win) is the big mover: he is up to fourth on 159, 83 points behind with eleven rounds left, and as the only man winning races in a hurry, his title defense is very much alive.

Verstappen's zero leaves him beached on 112 — his Sprint points were the only three Red Bull took all weekend until Lawson's sixth place made it nine. In the constructors' fight, Mercedes' second consecutive double podium stretched its lead to 87 points over Ferrari, with McLaren's 43-point haul the biggest of the weekend but still only third overall.

What's Next: Monza

After a weekend off, the season resumes at the Italian Grand Prix on September 4–6 — round 13 of 23, Ferrari's home race, and the scene of the Scuderia's next big push: the ADUO engine upgrade reportedly worth at least 15 hp.
