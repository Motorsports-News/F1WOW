The Most Dominant F1 Seasons Ever: Highest Win Percentages

Max Verstappen's 2023 romp sits atop the all-time list ahead of Alberto Ascari, Michael Schumacher and Jim Clark — the ten greatest single-season win rates in Formula 1 history, and the story behind each.

By F1wow Team 5 min read

Winning a single Formula 1 race is hard; winning the majority of them in a season is the stuff of legends. Across 75-plus years of world championship racing, only a handful of drivers have ever taken the chequered flag first in more than two-thirds of a campaign. Topping the list is Max Verstappen's 2023 season, whose 19 wins from 22 races set a mark that shattered a record which had stood for 71 years. Here is the all-time top 10 for single-season win percentage — verified against the official record books — and what makes each campaign special.

1. Verstappen 2023: A Record That Rewrote History

Max Verstappen's 2023 campaign is the gold standard of modern dominance — 19 wins from 22 races, an 86.36% hit rate. He sealed the record at that year's São Paulo Grand Prix, where his 17th victory in 20 races pushed him past a mark that had survived for seven decades, with two rounds still to spare. It was the capstone of a season in which he wrapped up the title with six races left and turned almost every weekend into a procession.

2. Ascari 1952: The 71-Year Benchmark

Before Verstappen, the name on this record was Alberto Ascari, whose 1952 season for Ferrari yielded six wins from eight races — 75.00%. It was a different era: the championship was only in its third year, the calendar was short, and the Indianapolis 500 (which the European runners largely skipped) counted toward the title. Ascari's sequence of nine consecutive wins across 1952 and into 1953 was, for its time, every bit as untouchable as it sounds, and his percentage stood unbroken for 71 years until Verstappen overhauled it.

The Modern Greats: Schumacher, Vettel, Hamilton

Three names dominate the modern, longer-calendar end of the list. Michael Schumacher's 2004 (13 from 18, 72.22%) is the benchmark of Ferrari's golden era, with his 2002 (11/17) only fractionally behind. Sebastian Vettel's 2013 — 13 wins from 19 races — bookended Red Bull's first spell of dominance with a run of nine straight victories to close the year. Lewis Hamilton's 2020, 11 from 17, sits equal with Schumacher's 2002 at 64.71% and capped the most dominant stretch of the Mercedes era. Verstappen also appears twice, his 2022 (15/22) bookending his own record-setting peak.

Jim Clark and a Different Era

The list is rounded out by Jim Clark, the Lotus legend who appears twice — 1963 (7/10) and 1965 (6/10) — alongside Juan Manuel Fangio's 1954 (6/9). Their percentages reflect a time when seasons were a fraction of today's length and a single car-driver-team combination could flatten the field for an entire year. Clark's 1963 campaign, in particular, is often cited as one of the most complete seasons any driver has ever produced. Modern calendars of 20-plus races make climbing this list considerably harder, which is precisely why Verstappen's 2023 number stands out.

Where Does 2026 Stand?

For context, the current season is being paced by Mercedes' Kimi Antonelli, whose six wins from the first eleven rounds of 2026 give him a 54.5% rate — the best in the field today but, at the halfway mark, short of the all-time top 10's 60% floor. To crash this list by season's end over a 23-round campaign he would need to win roughly eight of the remaining dozen races; a tall order, but the kind of second half that turns a strong season into a historic one.
