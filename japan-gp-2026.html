Japanese Grand Prix 2026: Kimi Antonelli Wins at Suzuka

Mercedes star claims victory at Suzuka as championship lead changes hands after dramatic race

By F1wow Team 5 min read

Kimi Antonelli delivered a stunning drive to win the Japanese Grand Prix 2026 at Suzuka Circuit, taking the lead in the Drivers' Championship. The Mercedes youngster secured his second victory of the season ahead of Oscar Piastri and Charles Leclerc in a dramatic race that shook up the championship standings.

Suzuka Circuit: The Driver's Playground

Suzuka Circuit is unlike any other track on the Formula 1 calendar. Its distinctive figure-eight layout, created by the famous crossover bridge, makes it one of the few truly classic circuits remaining in modern F1.

The 5.807-kilometer circuit features 18 corners that test every aspect of a driver's skill. From the legendary 130R, taken flat-out by the bravest drivers, to the technical Spoon Curve and the iconic final chicane (Degner), Suzuka rewards precision and bravery.

Key Sector Analysis:

Sector 1: Features the fast opening sequence leading into the Dunlop Curve and the challenging Degner corners. A good exit from Degner is crucial for the long straight that follows.

Sector 2: Includes the famous Spoon Curve and the ultra-fast 130R. This sector separates the brave from the cautious, with 130R being one of the most thrilling corners in F1.

Sector 3: The final sector includes the demanding Casio Triangle chicane and the final corner, where momentum is key for a strong lap time.

Championship Standings After Japan GP

Kimi Antonelli delivered a masterful performance at Suzuka to take the lead in the Drivers' Championship, with his second win of the 2026 season. The Mercedes youngster now leads teammate George Russell by 9 points after Russell finished fourth. Charles Leclerc completed the podium and moved up to third overall.

Key Storylines from Suzuka

Antonelli Ascends: Kimi Antonelli's victory at Suzuka wasn't just another race win—it was a statement. The Mercedes youngster showcased exceptional pace and maturity at one of F1's most demanding circuits, taking the championship lead from his teammate in the process. At just 20 years old, the Brazilian phenom is proving he's ready for title contention.

McLaren's Rising: Oscar Piastri delivered a stunning drive to finish P2, while Lando Norris capitalized on strong strategy to claim P5. McLaren's performance at Suzuka signals they're becoming genuine race winners. The Woking team's car has shown impressive pace across different circuit types, making them a threat for the remainder of the season.

Ferrari's Fight Back: Charles Leclerc's P3 and Lewis Hamilton's P6 showed Ferrari are improving with each race. The Scuderia gained valuable points and reduced the gap to Mercedes in the Constructors' Championship. However, both drivers will know they need more to challenge Mercedes consistently.

Red Bull's Struggle Continues: Max Verstappen could only manage P8 at Suzuka, continuing a frustrating start to 2026 for the four-time World Champion. The reigning champions are well adrift in both championships. Team principal Christian Horner faces a race against time to unlock performance.

Alpine Points: Pierre Gasly (P7) and Esteban Ocon (P10) secured valuable points for Alpine, showing the French team has turned a corner after difficult previous seasons.

Race Highlights

The Japanese Grand Prix delivered drama from start to finish. Kimi Antonelli made a perfect start from pole position and managed the opening laps flawlessly, building a gap that would prove crucial as the race developed.

Oscar Piastri made early gains from his starting position and settled into a strong rhythm. The McLaren driver's pace on the hard compound was exceptional, allowing him to finish the race without an additional pit stop—strategy that secured his second-place finish.

Charles Leclerc battled hard with George Russell throughout the race, eventually making a brilliant move at the Casio Triangle chicane to claim the final podium spot. The Ferrari driver's aggression was rewarded, while Russell had to settle for fourth.

Lewis Hamilton produced a mature drive to sixth, while Pierre Gasly and Esteban Ocon delivered a double-points finish for Alpine. Max Verstappen's frustrations continued as he could only manage eighth place.

Late Drama: A late-race safety car brought the field back together, setting up a frantic final few laps. Antonelli's restart was composed, and he controlled the gap to maintain his advantage to the checkered flag.

Tire Strategy in Action

Pirelli nominated the C1 (hard), C2 (medium), and C3 (soft) compounds for the Japanese Grand Prix, and teams deployed varied strategies throughout the 53-lap race at Suzuka.

Oscar Piastri's stunning P2 was built on a bold one-stop hard tire strategy that many teams deemed too conservative. The McLaren driver made the strategy work with exceptional tire management, capitalizing when others made their second pit stops.

Winner Kimi Antonelli ran a conventional two-stop approach (medium-hard-medium), managing his pace throughout while maintaining the gap to second place. Most of the top ten finishers used the two-stop strategy, highlighting the durability of the hard compound at Suzuka's abrasive surface.

Looking Ahead: Miami GP

Suzuka has delivered a pivotal moment in the 2026 championship. With Kimi Antonelli now leading the championship and Mercedes extending their Constructors' advantage, the pressure shifts to the rest of the grid as Formula 1 heads to Miami for Round 4 on May 1-3.

McLaren's strong performance in Japan suggests they'll be genuine contenders in Miami, while Ferrari will be working hard to close the gap. For Red Bull, the situation is becoming critical—Miami represents an opportunity to turn their season around before the championship gap becomes unmanageable.

The Miami International Autodrome is a completely different challenge to Suzuka. Its tight street circuit nature could favor different teams and drivers, potentially shaking up the order once again. One thing is certain after Suzuka: the 2026 season is shaping up to be one of the most competitive in recent memory.

Japanese GP History

The Japanese Grand Prix has been part of Formula 1 since 1976, with Suzuka hosting the majority of races. The circuit has witnessed some of the most dramatic moments in F1 history:

Iconic Moments:

• 1989: The infamous Senna/Prost collision that decided the championship

• 2005: Kimi Räikkönen's stunning victory from 17th on the grid

• 2011: Jenson Button's masterclass in wet conditions

• 2022: Max Verstappen clinched his first World Championship at Suzuka

• 2026: Kimi Antonelli's dominant victory to take the championship lead

Japanese fans are among the most passionate in the world, creating an incredible atmosphere. The grandstands filled with enthusiastic supporters waving flags and cheering for their favorite drivers make Suzuka a special place on the calendar.
