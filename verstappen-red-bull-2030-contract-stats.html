Four More Years: Max Verstappen Extends Red Bull Stay to 2030

Formula 1's biggest summer-break story is settled: Max Verstappen will wear Red Bull blue until the end of 2030. We chart the partnership in numbers - four titles, 71 wins, and a legacy still being written.

By F1wow Team 6 min read

ZANDVOORT WEEK, August 20 — The news broke from the source itself. Formula 1's official account posted the announcement to its millions of followers: "BREAKING: Max Verstappen will remain at Red Bull Racing until the end of 2030." Within hours the post had cleared 1.3 million likes and more than 8,500 comments — and every remaining thread of the summer's loudest silly-season rumour, a Verstappen-Mercedes move, was gone in a single sentence.

The Deal That Ends Every Rumour

For months, the story writes itself in every paddock conversation: Verstappen sixth in the championship, winless since who knows when, Red Bull no longer the dominant force of 2023 — and a Mercedes team winning everything with Kimi Antonelli and a rumoured seat open. The official announcement kills all of it. Verstappen stays. The earliest he could be anywhere else is 2031, by which point he will be 33.

The scale of the commitment is easy to miss. His previous deal, announced in 2022, already ran to the end of 2028 — with performance-related clauses that had kept Mercedes' hopes of prising him away alive right up to this summer. The new arrangement is a two-year extension on top of it, signed with the team utterly transformed around him: Helmut Marko, Adrian Newey and Christian Horner have all departed since his last title, and Laurent Mekies now runs the operation from Milton Keynes. It also completes the picture we mapped in our summer-break silly-season guide, where Verstappen's Red Bull future topped the list of open storylines. That box is now ticked — in red ink.

The Red Bull Highs

Eleven seasons, one promotion, and a highlight reel nobody in the paddock can match. The moments that built the partnership Red Bull has just extended to 2030:

2016

The Promotion That Changed Everything

Swapped from Toro Rosso to the senior team mid-season, the 18-year-old won the Spanish Grand Prix on his Red Bull debut — the youngest race winner in Formula 1 history, a record that still stands.

2021

The Last-Lap Crown

Ten wins, eight poles, and a first world championship decided on the final lap of the final race in Abu Dhabi — the most dramatic title finish the sport has produced.

2022

454

Fifteen wins and 454 points, then an all-time single-season record, with Charles Leclerc's runner-up campaign left trailing on 308.

2023

The Most Dominant Season Ever

19 wins from 22 races (86.36%), 21 podiums, 18 poles and ten consecutive victories — an all-time streak. It remains the benchmark season on our all-time win-percentage list.

2024

Title Number Four

A fourth consecutive championship, matching Sebastian Vettel's Red Bull-era streak of 2010–2013 — the only two drivers ever to win four in a row with the same team.

2025

The Reign Ends

Eight wins and 15 podiums kept the fight alive deep into the season, but Lando Norris took the crown — ending Verstappen's four-year run as champion.

2030

The Next Chapter

A winless 2026 so far, sixth in the standings — and Red Bull commit anyway, locking their franchise driver in through the end of 2030.

What the Paddock Says

The most telling reactions came from the three people at the centre of the story. Verstappen himself said he was "really pleased with the contract extension", thanked the team "for the trust they put in me", and called Red Bull "like a second family for me" — a place where "I feel very at home". Red Bull's statement said the team was "immensely proud", calling this "one of the most successful and defining partnerships in F1 history". And the man who now runs the operation gave the deal its fullest framing.

"His impact on our team and on Red Bull's motorsport story has been transformational, and he has been fundamental to everything we have accomplished together. The decision to continue our journey together is rooted in the trust Max and the team have built over many years, as well as Max's confidence in our people, our culture and our vision for the future."

Laurent Mekies — Red Bull team principal

"Everyone in Milton Keynes believes in what we are building and I am looking forward to the next chapter, fighting for more victories and competing for championships as we continue to shape the future of this team. To make this announcement during the last Grand Prix at Zandvoort is a great moment for me as well. Hopefully we can give the fans a special send-off for the final race."

Max Verstappen — on staying to 2030

"The Max to Mercedes thing for now is not on."

Toto Wolff — Mercedes team principal, earlier this season; it is now off for good

Wolff's line, given to the Press Association back in March, aged into permanent fact the moment the announcement dropped. Mercedes' "absolute priority" is George Russell and Kimi Antonelli, and the four-time champion is off the market until 2031 — the rumour mill's longest-running story is finally over. Mekies, meanwhile, is the third Red Bull boss Verstappen has answered to, having succeeded Christian Horner last summer, and the deal doubles as his endorsement: the champion staying is the loudest possible signal that he buys the vision.

The 2026 Reality Check

Now the honest part. The deal arrives in Verstappen's toughest season since 2016: sixth in the championship on 109 points, no grand prix wins, four podiums, and a 110-point deficit to Antonelli with 12 of 23 rounds left. Every pole this season has gone to a Mercedes or a McLaren — Antonelli six, Russell four, Norris one. The driver profile page tells the same story the timing screens do: the raw speed is intact, the car is not where he needs it.

Which is precisely why the contract matters. Red Bull is not extending a stat line; it is betting that the era of 2026's rules will swing back, and that the man who delivered four titles in the last era is the one to lead the next one. There will be change around him — his race engineer Gianpiero Lambiase is already confirmed to join McLaren from 2028 — but the driver seat at the centre of the project is now settled for half a decade.

The timing is a statement in itself: days before his home Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort, the final race under the current contract, announced on the eve of a sprint weekend in front of 100,000 orange fans. The immediate questions — can Red Bull salvage 2026, can Verstappen add a podium at home — get their first answers this weekend. The long question, the one about 2030, has just been answered for him.
